# SpaceDonkey

![Donkey This Ass To Space](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PaulTiffany/SpaceDonkey/main/assets/SpaceDonkey.png)

## Can we Donkey this ass to space?

Space is not mainly **up**. Orbit is mostly **sideways, very fast**.

Rockets carry their propulsion with them and do almost all of the work during one launch. A space elevator tries to replace that with one enormous continuous cable.

**SpaceDonkey asks a dumber question: what if we just keep swinging?**

Imagine a continuous ring wrapped around Earth at the equator. From that ring hang many powered swings — **Donkeys** — of different lengths, speeds, and phases.

A payload reaches one Donkey, rides it, lets go, flies freely, reaches another, and repeats. It does not have to keep climbing. It can coast, glide, or even dive if that makes the next handoff easier.

Each Donkey only needs to leave the payload somewhere the **next legal move** exists.

Eventually the payload lets go with enough sideways speed that it keeps missing Earth.

**That's orbit.**

## The whole research question

> **Does increasing the number of momentum-exchange stages create enough rendezvous grace, bounded acceleration, redundancy, and recovery to outweigh the added tether, control, reliability, and infrastructure burden?**

SpaceDonkey does **not** claim that the system is buildable, economical, safe, or superior to rockets. The point is to turn the joke into a sequence of falsifiable engineering questions.
