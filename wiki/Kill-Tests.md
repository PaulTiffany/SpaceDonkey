# Kill Tests

SpaceDonkey should be easy to kill.

A good version of the project does not protect the concept from bad news. It identifies the fastest experiments that can prove the architecture useless.

## K1 — The compression ring fails

If a closed self-supporting equatorial compression hoop cannot satisfy specific-strength, buckling, stability, thermal, construction, and failure-containment requirements at a useful radius, the current backbone hypothesis fails.

This is a first-class kill test, not a detail to be solved later.

## K2 — The capture corridor does not exist

If useful momentum transfer requires relative velocity, timing precision, or attachment shock so severe that no practical soft-capture region exists, the central **grace** thesis fails.

The key question is not whether two trajectories mathematically intersect. It is whether they remain close enough in state space for a physically survivable acquisition and lock.

## K3 — High stage count destroys reliability

If each handoff succeeds with probability $p$, a naive independent chain gives approximately

$$
p^N.
$$

That becomes terrible quickly for large $N$.

High stage count therefore requires one or more of:

- extremely high per-stage reliability,
- multiple reachable next Donkeys,
- recoverable missed handoffs,
- graceful free-flight fallback trajectories,
- and local failure isolation.

If the system cannot become a recoverable **network** rather than a brittle chain, multiplicity may hurt more than it helps.

## K4 — Atmosphere wins

Long lower tethers may suffer unacceptable:

- drag,
- heating,
- acoustic loading,
- weather,
- oxidation,
- lightning/electrical exposure,
- and uncontrolled interaction with air traffic or debris.

The first practical Donkey may have to begin above the dense atmosphere, leaving a conventional launch, airbreathing, or suborbital first stage below it.

That would not necessarily kill SpaceDonkey as an upper transport network, but it would kill the strongest ground-to-orbit version.

## K5 — Tether mass scales badly

Required $\Delta v$, radius, payload mass, strength, taper, fatigue margin, micrometeoroid tolerance, and safety factor may make each Donkey prohibitively massive.

A concept with elegant kinematics but impossible tether mass is dead.

## K6 — Local loads wreck the backbone

Even if the ring can support itself in an ideal static calculation, Donkey reaction loads may introduce unacceptable:

- bending,
- local buckling,
- resonant waves,
- torsion,
- fatigue,
- or destabilizing global modes.

The ring and Donkeys cannot be validated independently forever.

## K7 — Angular-momentum return has no credible path

Every outbound payload receives prograde angular momentum from somewhere.

If the architecture cannot return that momentum through inbound traffic, Earth coupling, propulsion, external reservoirs, or another explicit mechanism at useful throughput, repeated launches are not sustainable.

## K8 — Recharge throughput is too low

A Donkey may be efficient per event yet recharge too slowly for meaningful traffic.

The useful metric is not just joules per kilogram but kilograms per day at acceptable recovery cost.

## K9 — Debris and traffic make the geometry unsafe

A network of many long moving members creates large collision cross-section.

If avoidance, severability, redundancy, tracking, and safe failure cannot make that tolerable, the architecture fails even if nominal dynamics work.

## K10 — Bootstrap is economically impossible

A mature SpaceDonkey network could be excellent and still never exist if the first useful partial network requires an absurd amount of conventional launch mass.

The architecture needs a bootstrap sequence in which early infrastructure creates enough value to help build later infrastructure.

## K11 — More Donkeys do not actually buy grace

This is the most direct scientific failure mode.

Run one- and two-Donkey optimization. If smaller per-stage transfers do **not** create materially larger robust capture/release overlap, lower peak load, wider timing tolerance, or better recovery, then the central reason for SpaceDonkey's high stage count disappears.

That would be an excellent result: the idea would have been killed by a small model instead of a planetary megaproject.
